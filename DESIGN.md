# Luminara Storefront Direction

## Position

Luminara is a curated house for vintage-inspired statement jewellery. The first
edit combines eight decorative silhouettes with enamel, glass, resin, zircon and
metal-tone finishes at approximately US$35 to US$58. The primary customer is a
style-led shopper who mixes vintage or second-hand pieces with contemporary
clothes and uses jewellery to make a familiar outfit personal. The primary
action is to add a specific piece to the bag.

The core belief is **Taste is assembled**. A personal signature is built from
repeated choices, combinations and pieces worn often enough to become part of
the wearer.

Use "vintage-inspired" until the provenance of an item is confirmed. Production
location, handmade status, recycled content, edition sizes and firing processes
must not be published without evidence.

## Visual System

- Gallery paper: `#F4F2ED`
- Surface: `#E8E5DE`
- Ink: `#171513`
- Graphite rule/accent: `#6F6962`
- Quiet stone accent: `#665E56`
- Display: Cormorant Garamond
- Text and labels: Jost

Typography uses three reliable levels: editorial display from 48–134px,
reading copy from 14–16px, and functional metadata from 9.4–10.5px. Decorative
labels may track widely, but prices, filters, product categories, navigation and
purchase actions never fall back into unreadable microtype.

Photography leads. The interface is a neutral gallery-paper field with black
image galleries and no large saturated UI colour. Colour comes from the
jewellery itself. Large Cormorant display type and sparse uppercase Jost labels
create the hierarchy. Images remain uncropped where the jewellery silhouette
matters and move to macro crops where material is the subject.

Product photography is graded consistently at approximately 72–86% saturation
with slightly increased contrast. This quiets unrelated blue, red and warm-brown
backgrounds while preserving the colour differences between enamel, glass,
resin and metal. The black-and-white portrait remains the only fully
desaturated image.

Layouts favour wide margins, thin rules, generous vertical space and a small
number of strong alignments. Product images use one portrait ratio in the
Cabinet. Boxed controls, custom cursors and decorative horizontal type bands are
removed so the photography and merchandise remain the visual event.

## Page Rhythm

1. Static Pearl Hour portrait with direct product action.
2. Plain product proposition built around “Taste is assembled.”
3. First product edit with four clear names and prices.
4. Static object study of the clock face, beadwork and sculpted gold line.
5. One black-and-white portrait.
6. The Assembly: shape, surface and repetition explained through type.
7. Complete, filterable Cabinet of eight pieces.
8. Final purchase action and service information.

## Motion

The hero is a still photograph, not a video. On desktop it uses an asymmetric
text-and-image split; on mobile the product image fills the screen and the type
sits in a protected lower zone. GSAP provides one title entrance, a restrained
camera move and a single developing-frame reveal in the object study. Lenis
provides smooth scrolling. Reduced-motion users see the complete still image and
all copy without motion.

## Sound

The original ambient score is a warm 48-second chamber loop at roughly 80 BPM.
It uses major ninths, soft plucked figures, a restrained harmonic bed and glass
tones. Sub-bass is limited so it feels immersive without a physical rumble.
Music begins only after the first browser interaction and sits well below the
content. Product, menu and bag actions use quiet procedural chimes. A small
persistent sound switch controls the experience.

## Avoid

- Spinner or generic loading copy.
- Video playback, timers or pause controls in the hero.
- Oversized empty black screens with no product information.
- Reading-level difficulty caused by ornamental body type.
- Stock slogans, invented reviews or invented production claims.
